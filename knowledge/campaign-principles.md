# Campaign Principles

A campaign is a deliberate, time-bound send to a defined audience. Its defining characteristic is that
*the store* chooses the moment — unlike an automation, where the customer does.

## When a campaign is the right instrument

- The trigger is a date or a business event: launch, sale, restock, holiday, announcement
- The content is genuinely new and applies to many people at once
- The moment matters more than the individual's position in their own journey

## When it is not

- The trigger is a customer behaviour → that is an automation
- It would be sent repeatedly on a schedule to simulate a trigger → that is an automation with worse timing
- It applies to a handful of people → use a different instrument entirely

See [../rules/campaign-rules.md#C11](../rules/campaign-rules.md).

## The five decisions

Every campaign is these five decisions, in this order. Reversing the order is how campaigns end up
looking for an audience to justify content that already exists.

1. **Objective** — which term of the revenue equation this moves
2. **Audience** — who it applies to, and explicitly who it does not
3. **Offer or reason** — why act now, discount or otherwise
4. **Channel and timing** — where and when, based on consent and observed behaviour
5. **Content direction** — the angle, the proof, the single call to action

## Offers are not the only reason to act

Reasons to act now, roughly from cheapest to most expensive in margin:

- New — genuinely new product, restock, collection
- Access — early, exclusive, members-first
- Information — how to use it, what changed, what others chose
- Deadline — a real cut-off, real inventory, real seasonality
- Bundle — more value at protected margin
- Threshold — free shipping or a gift above a spend level
- Discount — direct margin transfer

Working down this list before reaching the last item is most of the difference between a healthy and an
unhealthy promotional calendar.

## Campaigns compete with each other

Two sends to the same audience in the same window do not add. They split attention and accumulate
fatigue. The marketing calendar is a shared resource with finite capacity.

## Expected outcome before send

A campaign without a stated expectation cannot be evaluated afterwards, and a campaign nobody evaluates
gets repeated verbatim next quarter. State what success looks like and how it will be measured.

## The post-send half

A campaign is not finished when it sends. Result, comparison to expectation, and what that implies for
the next one are part of the campaign. This is the input
[../skills/campaign-optimization/SKILL.md](../skills/campaign-optimization/SKILL.md) runs on.
