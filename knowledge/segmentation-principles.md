# Segmentation Principles

Segmentation is a tool with a cost curve. It improves results up to a point, then quietly degrades them.
Knowing where that point sits is most of the skill.

## What segmentation buys

Relevance. A message aimed at people it actually applies to converts better and fatigues less. Nothing
more mystical than that.

## What segmentation costs

- **Content** — each segment needs its own message, or the segment was pointless
- **Measurement** — smaller groups produce noisier results and slower learning
- **Maintenance** — definitions drift, overlap, and rot; someone must own them
- **Coverage gaps** — over-specified segments leave contacts in none of them
- **Collision** — more segments means more contacts qualifying for several at once

## The test that matters

> Would these two groups receive a *different message, offer, timing or channel*?

If no, they are one segment. This single question eliminates most proposed splits. See
[../rules/audience-rules.md#A3](../rules/audience-rules.md).

## Useful segmentation dimensions, roughly by strength

1. **Lifecycle stage** — almost always worth segmenting on; see [customer-lifecycle.md](customer-lifecycle.md)
2. **Purchase behaviour** — what and when they bought, how often
3. **Product and category affinity** — strong when the catalogue is diverse
4. **Value band** — AOV and LTV, when they change the offer
5. **Engagement recency** — governs cadence more than content
6. **Channel consent and preference** — a hard constraint, not a preference
7. **Price sensitivity** — useful where observable, dangerous where inferred
8. **Location** — only when it genuinely changes relevance
9. **Demographics** — weakest; usually a proxy for something measurable directly

## Diminishing returns

Each added condition narrows reach. The first two or three conditions usually carry nearly all of the
gain; the fourth and fifth mostly cost reach and clarity. When a segment becomes too small to produce a
readable result, the right move is to stop splitting and start personalising inside one send.

## Segmentation vs personalisation

They solve overlapping problems at different costs:

| | Use when |
|---|---|
| **Segmentation** | The *strategy* differs — different offer, timing, channel or objective |
| **Personalisation** | The strategy is the same and only the *details* differ — product shown, name, recommendation |

Most over-segmentation is personalisation attempted with the wrong instrument.

## Exclusion is segmentation too

Who you leave out often matters more than who you include. Recent purchasers of the promoted item,
contacts in a competing journey, contacts recently contacted, non-consented contacts. An audience
defined only by inclusion is incomplete.

## Segments decay

A segment built on a 90-day window means something different six months later. Definitions need review,
and audience sizes should be re-checked at plan time rather than trusted from a previous run.
