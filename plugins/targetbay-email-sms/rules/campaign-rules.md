# Campaign Rules

One-off and scheduled campaign decisions. Cited as `campaign-rules.md#C1`.

---

### C1. Read the calendar first.
Check `email_sms.marketing_calendar` for what is already scheduled in the window before proposing
anything. A campaign that collides with an existing send competes with it.

### C2. One campaign, one objective.
A campaign that promotes a launch, clears stock and asks for reviews does none of them. If a campaign has
two objectives, it is two campaigns or it is one campaign with one objective.

### C3. Do not duplicate an existing campaign.
If a campaign with the same objective, audience and offer already exists or recently ran, modify or repeat
it deliberately rather than creating a near-identical twin. See [global-rules.md#G5](global-rules.md).

### C4. Justify every discount.
A discount is margin spent to buy a behaviour. State which behaviour, and whether a non-discount lever —
early access, bundling, restock, content, free shipping threshold — would work instead. Discount-trained
audiences stop buying at full price.

### C5. Match offer depth to customer value, not to urgency.
Deep discounts to customers who would have bought anyway destroy margin. Where the platform can
distinguish them, reserve depth for the groups that need it.

### C6. Timing is a decision, not a default.
Send time follows the audience's observed engagement pattern and the store's own history where that data
exists. If it does not exist, say the choice is a default and make it testable.

### C7. Sequence, do not overlap.
Campaigns aimed at the same audience in the same period must be sequenced with deliberate spacing. See
[frequency-rules.md](frequency-rules.md).

### C8. State the expected outcome before sending.
Every campaign plan carries what success looks like and how it will be measured. Without it the campaign
cannot be evaluated afterwards, and the next plan repeats its mistakes.

### C9. Never schedule a send without confirming audience, content and window together.
Approving an audience is not approving a send. See [safety-rules.md#S3](safety-rules.md).

### C10. Prefer measurable variation over untestable novelty.
If a campaign is an experiment, structure it as one: one variable, a stated hypothesis, a readable sample.
See [../knowledge/experimentation-principles.md](../knowledge/experimentation-principles.md).

### C11. Campaigns do not replace automations.
If the trigger is a customer behaviour rather than a date, it belongs in an automation. Repeatedly sending
a manual campaign to simulate a behavioural trigger is a maintenance burden with worse timing.

### C12. Record dependencies.
If a campaign depends on inventory, a launch date, creative, or another campaign having run first, state
the dependency in the plan. Undeclared dependencies are what make marketing calendars collapse.
