# Member Lifecycle

How membership behaves over time, and what that implies for planning.

## Enrolment is not participation

A member who joined and never earned is not in the programme in any meaningful sense. The useful
population split is enrolled / earning / redeeming, and the drop between each pair is where programme
design shows up. A programme reported by enrolment count is reporting its least informative number.

## The first redemption is the activation event

Until a member has redeemed once, points are an abstraction. After, they are a currency they have used.
Time to first redemption is therefore the metric most worth compressing, and it is a function of
reachability rather than of communication volume.

## Quiet members leave the programme before they leave the store

A member whose earning slows is usually still buying, and a member whose earning stopped often stopped
buying some time earlier. The gap between the two makes the programme an early-warning system — but only
if activity is read relative to that member's own pattern rather than to a store-wide threshold.

## Tier movement is asymmetric

Members climb deliberately and fall passively. A member approaching a threshold responds to being told how
close they are; a member about to fall out of a tier responds to that far more strongly, and resents it
more if it happens silently. Both are timing decisions the programme can make and usually does not.

## Balance ageing tells a different story from balance size

A large balance accumulated recently is a member who is engaged and has not yet redeemed. A large balance
accumulated years ago is a member who has probably left, holding an obligation the store still carries.
The same number means opposite things, which is why ageing is read rather than totals
([../rules/economics-rules.md#E4](../rules/economics-rules.md)).

## Programme fatigue is real and is shared

Members receive tier updates, balance reminders, expiry warnings and reward announcements — and every one
competes with the store's ordinary marketing for the same attention and the same frequency budget. A
loyalty programme that treats its messaging as exempt because it is "service" is the most common cause of
loyalty-driven unsubscribes ([../rules/global-rules.md#G13](../rules/global-rules.md)).

## A programme's end state is worth naming

A mature programme is one where enrolment is automatic at the moment of first purchase, first redemption
happens within a plausible number of orders, redemption rate is stable, liability ages predictably, and
tier movement is communicated before it happens rather than after. Planning toward that produces different
choices from planning toward enrolment growth.
