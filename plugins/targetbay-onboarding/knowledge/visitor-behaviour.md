# Visitor Behaviour

How visitors move through a store, and what that implies for where personalization belongs.

## Entry context decides almost everything about the first screen

A visitor arriving from a branded search, a product ad, a comparison site and an email all want different
first screens, and all of that is knowable without identifying anyone. Entry source and landing intent are
the strongest anonymous signals available, and they are available immediately.

## Hesitation clusters in predictable places

The size selector, the delivery estimate, the returns policy, the price comparison against an alternative —
visitors stall in the same places, and those places are where proof, reassurance or a recommendation
changes an outcome. Personalization placed away from a hesitation point is competing with nothing.

## Depth signals intent better than duration

A visitor who viewed four products in one category is telling you more than one who spent six minutes on
one page, which might mean interest or might mean confusion. Sequence and breadth carry intent; time on
page mostly carries ambiguity.

## Cart state is the strongest live signal a store has

What is in the cart is a stated intention, currently held. It constrains what is worth recommending, what
must be excluded, and what an offer should be about — and it is available for anonymous visitors as
readily as identified ones.

## Search queries are the catalogue's own critique

Zero-result queries name what visitors believe the store sells and it does not — either a catalogue gap, a
vocabulary mismatch, or a merchandising failure. High-volume queries with low conversion name products the
store has but presents badly. Neither requires any visitor identification to act on.

## Returning visitors behave differently from returning customers

Someone who visited three times without buying is evaluating, and responds to reassurance. Someone who
bought and came back is replenishing or expanding, and responds to relevance. Treating both as "returning"
collapses two opposite intents into one segment.

## Repeat exposure to the same offer decays fast

The second showing of an offer performs far worse than the first, and the fourth is mostly an irritation
that trains visitors to dismiss without reading. This is why frequency caps are per visitor across all
surfaces rather than per campaign ([../rules/targeting-rules.md#T8](../rules/targeting-rules.md)).
