# Surface Rules

Constraints on placements and the pages that carry them. Cited as `surface-rules.md#U2`.

---

### U1. A surface hosts a decision. Name it.
Every placement sits in front of a specific visitor decision — which product, whether to add, whether to
proceed, whether to search again. A placement that does not is decoration and is rejected as such.

### U2. State what a new placement displaces.
Attention is finite ([global-rules.md#G7](global-rules.md)). Every addition names what moves down or comes
off, or states why the surface genuinely had room.

### U3. Match the strategy to the surface, not to the catalogue.
A product page hosts a substitution or complement decision; a cart hosts a completion decision; a category
page hosts a narrowing decision. The recommendation strategy follows from which of those the surface
serves, not from whichever algorithm is available.

### U4. Never recommend what the visitor already has.
Items in the cart, recently purchased, or out of stock are excluded at design time. This is the most
common and most visible personalization failure, and it costs credibility disproportionately.

### U5. Check cannibalisation before claiming incremental revenue.
A placement that moves a purchase from one product to another, or from one surface to another, has not
added revenue. Compare against what the surface produced before
([measurement-rules.md#M3](measurement-rules.md)).

### U6. A placement's failure mode is part of its design.
State what shows when the strategy returns too few results, when inventory is thin, or when the visitor is
anonymous and new. An empty or broken widget on a live page is worse than no widget.

### U7. Evaluate a surface on outcome, never on impressions.
Impressions measure placement, not effect. The measure is conversion, add-to-cart, revenue per session or
return rate for the pages carrying it, against those that do not.
