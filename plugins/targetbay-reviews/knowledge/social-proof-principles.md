# Social Proof Principles

Durable reasoning about why reviews change behaviour. Skills cite this for *why*; the decisions themselves
live in skills, and the constraints in [../rules/](../rules/README.md).

## Proof answers a specific doubt

A shopper does not read reviews to find out whether a product is good. They read to find out whether a
specific doubt they already hold is justified — will it fit, will it last, is the colour accurate, does
the difficult part work. Proof that does not speak to a live doubt is skipped, however positive.

This is why the useful unit is not the star rating but the *attribute*: the recurring subject the reviews
keep returning to. A product whose reviews all discuss sizing is telling you what the page needs to say.

## The first review is worth more than the fiftieth

Moving from zero reviews to a few changes a product from unverified to verified. Moving from many to
slightly more changes almost nothing. The consequence is that coverage — how many products have *any*
proof — is usually a better target than volume on products that already have some.

The exact shape of that curve is store-specific and must be read from the store's own data, never
asserted from a benchmark ([../rules/global-rules.md#G3](../rules/global-rules.md)).

## A perfect rating reads as a broken one

A distribution with no criticism at all invites the suspicion that criticism was removed. Mixed ratings
with substantive text are more persuasive than uniform praise, which is one of several reasons suppressing
negative reviews is both prohibited and counterproductive
([../rules/safety-rules.md#S4](../rules/safety-rules.md)).

## Recency carries information

Proof ages. A product whose last review is two years old reads as discontinued, and reviews written before
a formulation, supplier or packaging change may describe a product that no longer exists. Review velocity
is therefore a property worth maintaining, not a one-time campaign.

## Negative reviews have a job

A well-answered complaint demonstrates what happens when something goes wrong, which is precisely the risk
the hesitant shopper is pricing. This is why the reply is written for the next reader rather than the
reviewer ([../rules/response-rules.md#P4](../rules/response-rules.md)).

## Media raises the cost of doubt

A photograph from a customer resolves the doubts text cannot — actual colour, actual scale, actual fit on
a real person. It is also much harder to obtain, which is why it is asked for separately and later
([../rules/request-rules.md#R8](../rules/request-rules.md)), and why it is worth targeting at the products
whose doubts are visual rather than functional.

## Proof compounds only where it is seen

A review that exists in the platform and appears nowhere the shopper looks has no effect on anything.
Coverage, display and distribution are three separate problems, and a store can be healthy on one while
failing on another.
