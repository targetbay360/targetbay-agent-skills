# Placement Rules

Constraints on where proof is shown. Cited as `placement-rules.md#D2`.

---

### D1. Place proof where the decision is made.
Proof earns its place at the point of hesitation — the product page, the comparison, the cart — not
wherever there is empty space. A placement that does not sit in front of a decision is decoration.

### D2. Show the proof that matches the product, not the store.
A store-wide carousel on a product page answers a question nobody asked. Where
`reviews.product_coverage` shows a product has its own reviews, show those; where it shows none, decide
deliberately whether category-level proof helps or whether the honest answer is to show nothing yet.

### D3. Never present aggregate proof in a way the distribution does not support.
A star average shown without count or distribution overstates thin evidence. Four reviews are four
reviews, and displaying them as "4.8 stars" without the count is a misrepresentation under
[safety-rules.md#S2](safety-rules.md).

### D4. Reuse requires rights and attribution.
Before planning UGC reuse beyond the review widget — email, ads, social, the homepage — confirm rights
status through `reviews.ugc_media`. Unknown rights means not yet, not probably fine.

### D5. Measure a placement by what it changed, not by what it showed.
Impressions are not the outcome. A placement is evaluated on conversion, add-to-cart or return-rate
movement for the pages carrying it, against pages that do not.

### D6. Do not add a placement without deciding what it replaces.
Onsite space is finite and every added element costs attention. A new placement states what moves down or
comes off, or it states why the page genuinely had room.
