# Response Rules

Constraints on replying to reviews and on responding to a rating problem. Cited as
`response-rules.md#P2`.

---

### P1. Diagnose before responding.
A falling rating is located before it is answered — which products, which attributes recur in the text,
which period, and what changed just before it. Read `reviews.review_content` and
`reviews.ratings_analytics`. A response strategy written without the cause is a guess in public.

### P2. Reply to the pattern, not to every review.
Replying to all of them is neither useful nor maintainable. Reply where a reply changes something: a
factual correction, a fixable problem, a question other buyers will also have. Volume of replies is not a
metric.

### P3. A reply is public and permanent.
Every merchant reply is `high_impact` under [safety-rules.md#S5](safety-rules.md), approved individually
under [#S10](safety-rules.md), and drafted as text a human reads before it is published — never generated
and posted in one step.

### P4. Never dispute the customer's experience.
A reply may correct a fact, explain a policy, or offer a resolution path. It may not tell a customer they
are wrong about what happened to them. The audience for a reply is the next reader, not the reviewer.

### P5. Move the resolution off the review.
Offer the route to a fix — support channel, replacement, refund policy — without negotiating it in the
thread. A reply that becomes a conversation is a support ticket in the wrong place.

### P6. Never offer compensation in exchange for changing or removing a review.
Offering to fix the underlying problem is legitimate. Conditioning anything on the review being edited,
raised or deleted is prohibited by [safety-rules.md#S3](safety-rules.md).

### P7. A recurring complaint is a product finding, not a reply problem.
When the same attribute appears across reviews of one product, the output is a product, listing or
fulfilment finding — surfaced as such — not a better-worded apology repeated thirty times.

### P8. Do not plan a rating recovery that depends on new reviews outvoting old ones.
Diluting a low rating with volume is slow, often fails, and does nothing about the cause. Volume is
proposed only alongside the fix for what produced the low ratings, and the expected recovery is stated
with its arithmetic, not asserted.
